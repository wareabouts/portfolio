# How Alex writes

The reference for any copy on alexfiel.com, whether Alex writes it or Claude drafts it from
notes. Three kinds of entry: what the existing pages show (observed), what Alex has confirmed
(rule), and what Alex has rejected, quoted, with the reason. Add to it every time Alex gives
feedback on a draft. Started 2026-09-03.

## The voice, observed on pages Alex wrote

Conversational and aimed at the reader. Questions are asked and answered: "What's a project
that we could complete in three days, that would be fun to interact with at a two day art show
the following weekend?" and "You following the logic?.... No? Me either, I just think it's
hilarious for some reason."

Ellipses mark a pause or a turn, not trailing off: "So... I designed and built a costume for my
vacuum", "Again... overcomplicated.", "We'd already shrunk down Cow Palace for the diorama...
why not shrink it one more time?"

Capitals for emphasis, one word at a time and not often: "sips power, so ideally won't need to
be charged for a LONG time", "It worked ENOUGH for the event itself", "solved power AND
connected to my PC directly".

Exclamation marks when something went well, not as decoration: "Everything came together
right before the show!", "They were a hit."

Concrete over abstract. Parts, materials, numbers, prices, vendors: "the cheapest pair of RC
cars that would ship next-day on Amazon", "two 0603 SMD LEDs", "a 15ft MicroUSB cable",
"sealed into the base with E6000 and some Flex-Seal", "more than 130k views and almost 6k
likes".

Overcomplicating and then landing on the simple thing is a story he tells about himself, with
the fix as the punchline: "I drafted designs for all kinds of different 3D printable
releases... and then I realized that the default servo arm would work."

Short paragraphs, one to three sentences, and a one-line paragraph when a beat needs it.

Results sections are candid about what fell short and end looking forward: "The only bummer
was battery life... nothing gave us more than 15min at a time", then "I got home from the trip
and bought a four pack of metal stanchions... V2's on the way..."

Jokes are about the absurdity of the project, never about the craft. The craft is described
straight.

## Rules Alex has confirmed

From copy round 1, 2026-09-02, all accepted:

- Titles are lowercase. Acronyms stay uppercase (ATLAS, ITLL, RGB, MSLA, CSS, UI, VR, 3D, LED,
  OS26). A brand whose casing is part of the name keeps it (OpenAI, ImageGen, FCView, B-Dubs).
  Other brands are lowercase (ellumen, glowforge, open sauce).
- Sections are `concept`, `process`, `results`, lowercase, in that order. `reception` or
  `extensions` can follow when there is something to say. Pages under about 120 words get no
  section headings.
- Sub-headings are sentence case and do not end in a colon. They keep proper nouns and their
  own wording ("Week 5: 2/4 -> 2/11" stays).
- First person, past tense. "We" where it was a team.
- Open with what it is. The first sentence says what the thing is in plain words before any
  backstory.
- Mechanics: its/it's, straight apostrophes inside any rewritten paragraph, "spray paint" not
  "spray-paint", Moon capitalized as a place.

From 2026-09-03:

- A page does not need a results section when the work speaks last. The show intros page ends
  on its third video.
- Captions describe what is in the image in one or two sentences, in the same voice, and say
  what a viewer could not know from looking (ring means zone, fill means status).

## The competence line

Feedback, 2026-09-03: "there's a fine line between honestly reflecting on challenges of a
project and sounding unprepared."

Challenges belong on the page when they are constraints of the world (Figma's export ceiling,
how mounting a sign in a real venue differs from the plan, working with volunteers) or things
found and fixed, described plainly and once. Nothing is dramatized, and no difficulty is
invented to make a paragraph land.

Rejected: "The team started using it for real when it was maybe 60% done, which kept things
exciting." The team did start early, which is a fact worth stating. "Kept things exciting"
made it sound like a scramble.

Rejected: "subtracting them per venue double counted them, which I found out the hard way."
Not what happened. No mistaken order was placed and the rollout was fairly flawless. The rule
can be stated without a war story: signs already owned are subtracted once across both venues,
not once per venue.

## Say the real reason

Rejected, show intros: "So each one borrows a craft that reads instantly, a tabletop diorama,
a claymation set, a collage of cut-up public domain art, and spends the time on the few
details that sell it." A generic design rationale. The real reason: Open Sauce and Sauce+ are
built around makers, so the core style across the intros is visible materials.

When a draft explains why something was done, the explanation has to come from Alex. If the
notes do not say why, ask, or leave it out.

## Use Alex's sentences

When the notes already contain the sentence, use it. Alex's "In particular, I enjoyed tweaking
the lighting of the string lights in the campfire scene to get the glow I wanted, even as the
scene rotates quickly past the camera" replaced "The part I keep coming back to is the string
lights in the background of the camping scene." The original was more specific and more his.

## What reads as machine-written to Alex

- Emotional color the notes did not supply: "kept things exciting", "found out the hard way".
- Summary lines that tie a bow: "It was load-bearing", "Nothing gets re-entered anywhere",
  "the thirty-second demo", "the final code is the simplest of the three tries".
- Dramatized process: "live surgery", "every schema decision was surgery".
- Even rhythm, every sentence the same length, and tidy groups of three.
- Fragments used as punches. Alex uses fragments, but as asides with an ellipsis, not as a
  drumbeat.

## Drafting from notes

- Every claim in a draft traces to the notes, the media, or a fact that can be checked. Details
  taken from a screenshot rather than the notes get flagged in the report.
- Length follows the shape budgets in ROADMAP.md: a project is 150 to 400 words, a feature up
  to about 1,000. Shorter wins when the material allows it.
- Before an image ships, look at it for teammate names, emails, order totals and tokens. Blur
  them in a copy and keep the untouched original beside it with an underscore prefix.
- Read the LED panel studio page for the register that landed first time.
