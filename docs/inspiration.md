# Portfolio sites worth learning from

Fourteen personal sites by people doing work close to Alex's: hardware with software inside,
3D and fabrication, installations, VR, motion. Gathered 2026-09-03 from about sixty
candidates; every URL below loaded that day. Agencies, template galleries, and sites that
hide the work behind a mechanic were dropped. The rejects are at the end, because what they
get wrong is as useful as what the picks get right.

## The list

### 1. Bjorn Karmann

https://bjoernkarmann.dk

Designer and technologist; Paragraphica (context-to-image camera), Project Alias, Terraform
Table.

Home is a two-column chronological list: two thumbnails on the left, year, title, and a
one-line description on the right, and the only nav is his name and "About". A project page
opens with the year, a two-sentence summary and a "Try the camera" button, then about thirty
images and one video alternating with short paragraphs, a specs list, an embedded tweet, and a
"Selected works" thumbnail row at the bottom. Open Sans, small type, wide margins, nothing else.

Relevance: physical objects with embedded software, presented as products, with the exact
"summary first, then pictures and short text" rhythm Alex's case-study pages want.

### 2. Tore Knudsen

https://www.toreknudsen.dk

Interaction designer; IoT, machine learning, tangible computing (Pour Reception, Project
Alias, Digital Body Language).

Dark ground, chronological list with the year in the margin, one large photo, title, a short
paragraph and keyword tags per entry; nav is "work / about". Project pages put a hero photo
first, then the title with a compact metadata block (year, technologies, awards,
collaborators) and an itemized tools list (Processing, Arduino, Wekinator), a Vimeo embed, and
seven photos interleaved with 400 to 500 words.

Relevance: the tag-per-project and tools-per-project pattern is a clean way to label ESP32,
R3F, Blender and VR work without a skills page.

### 3. Neil Mendoza

https://www.neilmendoza.com

Artist and creative technologist; robotic and kinetic installations (Antivanity Mirror, The
Electric Knife Orchestra).

Home is a four-column grid of square photos (about forty projects) with an All / Art /
Commercial / Experiments filter and four nav words. A project page is a two-column header
(title and category left, 60 to 80 words right), one Vimeo embed with title and byline hidden,
a few full-width photos, then previous/next links. Quicksand, one weight, no color beyond
links.

Relevance: the closest model for documenting a physical installation with one video, a few
photos and very little text, and the filter is a good answer to mixing client work with
personal pieces.

### 4. Marcelo Coelho

https://cmarcelo.com

Designer at MIT; installations and products (Sandcastles with Vik Muniz, Beyond Vision).

Full-bleed hero, then a grid of about eight tiles with title and a category word (Artwork,
Installation, Product); nav is Work / Research / About. Project page: hero image, title,
one-line subtitle, about 80 words, two or three more full-width images. That is the whole page.

Relevance: shows how little a replica or prop page needs when the photos are good; a reference
for the shortest version of Alex's template.

### 5. Mohit Bhoite

https://bhoite.com

Engineer and circuit sculpture artist; LED and microcontroller freeform sculptures.

The sculptures index is a documentation-site layout: years in a left sidebar, one intro
sentence, a photo grid, and a theme toggle. Each sculpture page has a hero photo, a
first-person intro, a right-hand table of contents (Parts list, Tools, Block diagram,
Controller, Display), full-width photos between sections, a pin-connection table, a GitHub
link and previous/next. Pages run to several thousand words and the TOC makes that fine.

Relevance: the best example found of LED and microcontroller builds documented as build logs,
and the docs-site structure would suit the LED panel or wiggle lens pages if they get long.

### 6. Sophy Wong

https://sophywong.com

Designer; wearable tech, 3D-printed costumes and spacesuits, Make: magazine.

Work page is a hero photo, a one-line tagline, and a grid of photo tiles with titles; nav is
Work / About / Studio Notes / Resources. Project pages are title, about 150 words in the first
person, full-width photos separated by rules, and previous/next. One page says "Design & build
process (coming soon)", which is a reasonable way to publish before the process section
exists.

Relevance: 3D-printed props and costume enclosures, photographed against plain backgrounds,
written casually in the first person.

### 7. Arvind Sanjeev

https://arvindsanjeev.com/projects.html

Design technologist; Ghostwriter (AI typewriter), SPIN (AI music synthesizer), Kolam.

Projects page is a mosaic of thumbnails behind the title, then tiles with title and discipline
tags ("Artificial Intelligence | Physical Computing"), grouped Technology and Education. A
project page opens with the title and a hero GIF, press logos, then headings "The Intro", "The
Zine", "The Process", "The Learnings", 800 to 900 words in the first person, image carousels
for before/after shots, and embedded tweet threads. No bottom navigation, which is the one gap.

Relevance: intro, process, learnings is Alex's concept, process, results with different labels,
and the projects are restored hardware with software inside.

### 8. Amanda Ghassaei

https://amandaghassaei.com

Creative technologist; computational design and fabrication (brass CNC orrery, origami

Home is a single-column list: big image, uppercase title with date range, two or three
sentences with inline links. Project pages have the title and date range, a left rail of
external links (Instructables, build process, CAD model, GitHub) plus related projects, and

Relevance: the same mix of machined physical objects and browser tools, and the external-links
rail is the right place for the open-source files Alex publishes.

### 9. Itay Niv

https://www.itayniv.com

Interactive designer and creative technologist, ITP 2019; AR, voice, machine learning,

Monospace type, a left sidebar with Work / Video Reel / About, a two-sentence hello, and a
three-column grid of cards with title and tags. Project pages stack media on the left (hero

Relevance: closest in scale and voice to an early-career creative technologist; the
narrow-text-beside-media layout suits case studies that are mostly pictures.

### 10. Mike Tucker

https://www.mike-tucker.com

Interactive director; AR and VR at Magic Leap, Snap and Apple (Tonandi with Sigur Ros,

Dark, monospace, an intro paragraph with inline links, then a grid of looping GIF thumbnails.
Project pages use a breadcrumb title ("Mike Tucker @ Universal Everything / PolyFauna v2"),

Relevance: VR and spatial work shown through motion instead of stills, without any WebGL on

### 11. Scott Garner

https://scottmadethis.net

Artist and technologist; interactive museum pieces (The Musical Chair for the Children's

Home is a dense grid of square thumbnails, newest first, with Work / About / Store. A project

Relevance: the hardware/software heading split maps directly to the LED panel and exhibit

### 12. Blair Neal

https://ablairneal.com

Technologist and artist (Fake Love / The New York Times, Deeplocal); installations, author of

Full-bleed hero video with his name, then a card grid split into Professional work and

### 13. Charlotte Dann

https://www.charlottedann.com

Creative technologist at GitHub; generative design, fused glass, ceramics, jewelry.

Dark theme with a toggle, serif titles, an intro paragraph with highlighted keywords beside a

### 14. Kelli Anderson

https://kellianderson.com

Designer and paper engineer; This Book is a Planetarium, This Book is a Camera (MoMA), Russ &

Home is a numbered list ("no. 01", title, a three-sentence excerpt, "Case study") with a

Relevance: physical objects photographed and written up with results ("in more than 100,000
homes"), and one of them is a planetarium.

## Patterns that recur

- The home page is the index. Work starts at the top with at most one sentence of intro, and
  the nav is two to four words (Work / About; Portfolio / About; Work / Research / About).
  Nobody leads with a resume.
- A tile carries exactly three things: image, title, one descriptor. The descriptor is a
  category word (Mendoza, Coelho), discipline tags (Sanjeev, Niv, Knudsen), or a one-line
  description (Karmann). Year sits on the tile or as a group header (Bhoite, Knudsen), never
  in a separate archive.
- Project pages open with one hero (photo, GIF, or a single Vimeo embed with title and byline
  hidden) and a two- or three-sentence summary before any heading. Metadata sits in one
  compact block near the top (Knudsen: year, tech, awards, collaborators) or one line at the
  foot (Niv: date plus disciplines), not a sidebar.
- Headings name the story, not a framework: "The Intro / The Process / The Learnings",
  "Description / The Hardware / The Software", "Project origins / Fused glass". Body copy is
  80 to 900 words; only Bhoite goes longer, and adds a table of contents when he does.
- Images are column width and stacked, alternating with text. Carousels appear only for UI
  screenshots. Motion is a looping GIF or one embed; no autoplaying sound, no lightboxes.
- Tools and parts are named plainly in the text or a short list (Processing, Arduino,
  Wekinator; XIAO RP2040, FluidSynth), and open-source projects get an external-links rail
  (Instructables, CAD, GitHub).
- Every project page ends with a way onward: previous/next, or a "Selected works" thumbnail
  row. Sanjeev is the one exception and it is the thing his site is missing.
- Type is one sans (Open Sans on both Danish sites, Quicksand, a monospace) or one serif for
  titles (Dann, Anderson), small, with wide margins and no accent color beyond links. First
  person is the norm for people who build their own things; third person shows up on artists'
  sites and reads colder.
- Client work and personal work are kept apart on the same page, by filter (Mendoza), section
  (Neal), or grouping (Sanjeev). None of them hide the personal work.

## What this suggests for alexfiel.com

Most of the site already matches: a grid as the home page, first person, images at column
width, one accent used only for links. The gaps, in order of how cheap they are to close:

- A way onward at the foot of every project page. Previous and next in grid order, or a row of
  three related covers. The site has neither.
- A one-line descriptor per tile. The grid shows title and year; every strong site shows what
  the thing is in a few words. That is a `summary` field in front matter, which the desk could
  ask for, and it doubles as the meta description.
- Tools named in one place. A short `tools` list in front matter (Blender, ESP32, React Three
  Fiber) rendered as tags under the title, instead of hoping the prose mentions them.
- A links rail for the open-source pieces: repo, live site, files. Today those are inline
  links in the last paragraph.
- Story headings versus concept / process / results. The framework labels keep the pages alike,
  which Alex asked for; the sites here use headings that say what happened. A middle path is
  keeping the three sections but letting a sub-heading under each say the specific thing.
  Worth deciding once, not per page.
- Client and personal work told apart. The categories are by medium; nothing marks a piece as
  made for Open Sauce or Ellumen versus made for fun. A `for` field, shown in the metadata
  line, would do it.

## Anti-patterns from the rejects

- Experience-first home pages: laurens.art is a 3D climbing wall you scroll to reveal projects
  and it has to print "use your mouse wheel or arrow keys"; bruno-simon.com is a driving game
  with no plain project pages. Both hide the work behind a mechanic.
- Image dumps without writing: felixfaire.com has a full-bleed particle hero and project pages
  that are a gallery plus a "Full credits" link, so you cannot tell what he did on any of them.
- Text-only lists linking off-site (nicole.pizza, kylemcdonald.net, bod.ge, mattdesl.com): fine
  for well-known people, but there is nothing to browse and the projects live elsewhere.
- Marketing above the work: andreasrefsgaard.dk now titles itself "AI keynote speaker" with
  "Book a talk" buttons before the projects; sophywong.com runs a promo banner across the top.
- Shop or blog as the portfolio: ivanmiranda.com is Shopify cards marked "Sold out";
  xylafoxlin.com and beckystern.com are chronological posts, so projects are dated entries
  instead of a curated set.
- Resume walls: birdinthecity.com opens with four paragraphs of "About me" (it even jokes about
  SEO keywords) and then lists client projects with tag clouds and no pages.
- Platform noise: jameshosken.com and driesdepoorter.be put a cookie banner over the work;
  Hosken's page title is "Home" and there is a search box in the nav. dwbowen.com repeats a
  40-item sidebar on every project page.
- Essays without a summary: andycavatorta.com's project pages run to ten headings before you
  learn what the machine is. Two-sentence pages (halfdanj.dk, Depoorter) have the opposite
  problem: no process at all.
- Decoration over structure: kahoabe.net wraps a good archive in a patterned border and an
  inline year-prefixed text list; mitxela.com uses image files for titles and category buttons.
- Dead maintenance: simonerebaudengo.com is now a casino affiliate page (lapsed domain);
  leahbuechley.com and robseward.com have expired certificates; jamesgeorge.org is
  self-signed; kennakagaki.com does not resolve (his site is ken-nakagaki.com);
  tiffanytseng.com belongs to a realtor. Renew the domain and the cert, and keep old URLs
  redirecting.

Also considered but not listed: Jonathan Bobrow (jonathanbobrow.com, one long page of hero
image, title, role and year, and a paragraph per project, good but not a grid); Reza Ali
(syedrezaali.com, 3D tools and WebXR, dark card grid with a carousel per project); Georgi
Nikolov (georgi-nikolov.com, software only, year and technology filters); Jie Qi
(technolojie.com, WordPress card grid, third person).
